## Answer

The platform is a **regulated U2U service** under OSA 2023 — the Schedule 1 educational exemption does not apply to an open-access platform.

**Age assurance (HEAA)**: not automatically mandated for purely educational content. Required only if the children's risk assessment concludes the platform disseminates primary priority or priority harmful content at medium-to-high risk. The concept spec must commit to completing that risk assessment; HEAA is conditional on its outcome. Acceptable HEAA methods if required: photo-ID matching, facial age estimation, MNO age checks, digital identity services. Self-declaration is unacceptable.

**Content moderation**: three risk assessments are legally required (all deadlines now past — must be completed before launch): illegal content risk assessment, children's access assessment, children's risk assessment. Proportionate measures to prevent priority illegal content must be implemented. A reporting mechanism and complaints process are mandatory regardless of risk level.

**Teacher/creator identity**: no statutory DBS or identity verification requirement in OSA. However, the children's risk assessment is likely to identify unverified adult access to minors as a risk requiring mitigation — voluntary teacher identity verification (ID check + self-declaration of DBS status) is a practical near-requirement for a compliant risk posture.

**Ofcom registration**: not required unless platform reaches £250M qualifying worldwide revenue.

**ICO Children's Code (UK GDPR)**: significant data design obligations from day one — DPIA required before launch; high-privacy defaults (profiling off, geolocation off, public profiles off); minimum data collection; parental consent for under-13s; child's own consent for 13–17s; no nudge techniques; age-appropriate transparency.

## Question

What obligations does the UK Online Safety Act 2023 (and related guidance) impose on a platform that:
- is accessible to under-18s (UK school students)
- allows adults to publish content that minors can subscribe to and consume
- has no direct messaging between users
- is not school-scoped (any learner can subscribe to any teacher)

Specifically needed for the concept spec:
1. Does the platform require **age assurance** (age verification or age estimation) for under-18 users?
2. What **content moderation** obligations apply to teacher-published content?
3. What obligations apply to the **teacher/creator side** (identity verification, DBS equivalent)?
4. Is there an Ofcom **registration or notification** requirement for a platform of this type?
5. Are there **GDPR/UK GDPR** obligations specific to child users beyond standard GDPR (e.g. Children's Code / Age Appropriate Design Code)?

Sources: Ofcom guidance, ICO Children's Code, KCSIE (for school context), OSA 2023 legislation.
